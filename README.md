# 5-Extension-of-Vibrating-Screen-Mesh-Life-Through-Mechanical-Modification
Project Overview

A recurring mesh failure problem was observed in the vibrating screen, where screen meshes were frequently failing after approximately 500 running hours. The repeated failures resulted in production stoppages, maintenance intervention, and manpower engagement for mesh replacement and related maintenance activities.

A mechanical modification and installation methodology was implemented to improve mesh stability, reduce excessive vibration, and prevent damaging metal-to-metal contact.

Problem

The vibrating screen meshes were experiencing premature failure with an average service life of around 500 hours.

Major impacts:

* Frequent mesh failures
* Production stoppage during mesh replacement
* Repeated maintenance intervention
* Additional manpower engagement
* Increased maintenance frequency
* Reduced equipment reliability

Root Cause / Observations

During inspection and installation activities, excessive mesh movement and uneven vibration were observed in certain sections of the screen.

Direct contact between the mesh/support arrangement and the steel stringer frame was also contributing to mechanical impact and vibration transmission.

The fixing arrangement was therefore modified to improve mesh support and control unwanted movement.

Engineering Solution

1. Flat Rubber Installation

Flat rubber strips were installed on the stringer frames to prevent direct metal-to-metal contact.

The rubber layer provided cushioning between the mesh and supporting structure, helping to reduce impact, friction, and vibration transmission.

2. Improved Mesh Fixing Using J-Hooks

J-hooks were installed to properly secure and restrain the mesh.

The fixing arrangement was optimized to prevent excessive mesh movement during screen operation while maintaining the required tension and support.

3. No-Load Vibration Trial

After completing mesh installation, a no-load trial was conducted before putting the screen into normal operation.

The mesh was visually monitored during operation to identify:

* Uneven vibration
* Excessive movement
* Loose sections
* Higher vibration zones at either end of the mesh

4. Additional J-Hook Provision

Where higher vibration or excessive movement was observed at a particular section, additional J-hooks were provided to improve restraint.

This trial-and-correction approach helped achieve a more stable mesh arrangement before full-load operation.

Result

* Mesh service life increased from 500 to 2,500 running hours — a 5× improvement.
* Premature and recurring mesh failures were eliminated.
* Production stoppages due to mesh failure were eliminated.
* Routine manpower engagement for mesh replacement and related maintenance was eliminated.
* Improved mesh stability and vibration behaviour during screen operation.
* Reduced maintenance frequency and equipment downtime.
* Improved overall vibrating screen reliability and availability.

Key Achievement

Increased vibrating screen mesh life from approximately 500 hours to 2,500 hours — achieving a 5× improvement in service life.

The improvement eliminated recurring mesh-related production stoppages and significantly reduced maintenance intervention and manpower requirements.

Engineering Benefits

* Improved vibrating screen reliability
* Increased mesh service life
* Reduced unplanned production stoppages
* Reduced maintenance frequency
* Reduced manpower requirement for mesh-related maintenance
* Improved mesh stability and vibration behaviour
* Improved overall equipment availability

Key Engineering Learnings

This project demonstrated the importance of looking beyond the mesh material itself and addressing the mechanical interface, fixing arrangement, and vibration behaviour of the complete screening system.

The combination of rubber cushioning, improved mechanical fixing, no-load vibration observation, and corrective J-hook installation provided a simple and practical reliability improvement without major equipment modification.
